Ten levels: what each week added to your bot

The course was a game with unlock levels. Each week gave the bot one new component and gave you one idea to keep. Unlock the levels in order and watch the rack on the right fill up, one module per week.

What to notice

No week stands alone. The objects of Week 1 are what the loop of Week 2 schedules, the gateway of Week 3 feeds them, and the backtester of Week 4 replays the tape they produce. Week 5 added no code: it tested that the bot survives the risk rules unattended. The last module is not a feature at all: it is the gate that lets every other module change without breaking the running bot.

Remember

Ten modules: connection, objects, event loop, gateway, backtester, risk rules, fast book, research pipeline, model, gate. In your bot: you can name the week and the reason behind every module, and that is the answer to most interview questions.

PART 2 / 4
The centerpiece

The whole bot, running: from a byte to the leaderboard

Twelve stations, in the order a live tick and the order it causes cross them. Click any station to read what it does, which week taught it, the trap, and the interview question about it. Then break it on purpose.

What you are looking at

The bot as a pipeline. Each glowing dot is one tick that becomes a decision, an order and a fill. Each box is a station with the week that built it. The panel at the bottom right is the bot's health: an equity curve (a schematic shape, not real data) and a list of what is going wrong. The red buttons switch off one lesson at a time.

Try this

  1. Watch a few dots go from Market data to Leaderboard. Click Risk checks and read its four blocks.
  2. Press Send a crash with the kill switch armed. Watch the dots stop at the risk station.
  3. Press Kill switch off, then Send a crash again. Watch the red dots pass and the curve dive.
  4. Try Stale data, Latency, Look-ahead leak and Fee blowup one at a time. Find the station that turns red each time.
  5. Press Heal all. Use Pause if you want to read a frozen frame.

    What to notice

    Every break leaves the socket open and the other stations green, and the bot still loses. Stale data and latency fail quietly: the dots keep moving, they just carry old prices. The leak does not hurt the live bot at all; it hurts you, because the backtest line lies and you ship the wrong idea. The fee blowup is the Week 0 arithmetic: at the engine's default schedule a taker pays $15 on $10,000 of notional to capture a $2 spread (interview bank MKT-04). The kill switch is the only break that changes how bad a bad day gets.

    Remember

    Byte, gateway, loop, book, features, signal, risk, order, routing, match, P&L, score: say the chain and the reason for each station. In your bot: you own the middle of it, from on_tick to the order, and the kill switch runs first.

    What to notice

    The biggest constellations are not about trading ideas. They are about the wire, speed, data and shipping: the engineering that lets a trading idea survive contact with a live market. In the ML theme, most cards are about honest measurement (leakage, walk-forward, baselines, drift), not about fancier models.

    Remember

    A technique is worth what you can explain about it: what it is, when it fails, and the number the course measured. In your bot: for every card, you can point at the line of code where you used it, or say why you did not need it.

    PART 4 / 4
    The boss fight

    Fifteen questions against the market

    One question from every theme of the interview bank, plus a few that came up every week. Each right answer is a hit. A wrong one costs nothing but shows you the answer and the week to reread.

    What you are looking at

    The market on the left, with fifteen hit points. On the right, one multiple-choice question at a time, drawn from the course's interview bank (the question id is shown). The squares under the boss are your answers so far: green right, red wrong. Your best score stays in this browser only.

    Try this

    1. Read the question and pick an answer with the mouse or the keys 1 to 4.
    2. Read the feedback, even when you were right: it carries the number from the course.
    3. Press Next (or Enter) and fight through all fifteen.
    4. On the result screen, open the weeks you missed, then press Fight again. The answers are shuffled.

    What to notice

    Most wrong answers on this list sound reasonable: that is why they are interview questions. The right answer almost always names a measurement (a split in time, a tail percentile, the fee against the spread) instead of an opinion.     Remember
