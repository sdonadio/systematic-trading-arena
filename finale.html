Act 3 · The money

Where the money went: trading, fees, carry, venues, IPOs and the shop

Why the money matters for the score

Your score only sees your net worth every 60 ticks. Everything that moves it counts: fees, borrow and margin carry, cancel fees, shop purchases, IPO shares, and the stock you held while the market moved.

What to notice

For most teams the biggest flow is not fees: it is what the stock they held did while they held it. The fee and carry flows are real money, but they are small next to a block of inventory caught in a falling market.

Remember

Inventory is a position. Every share you hold at the open is a bet on the market until you sell it, and every fee is certain while the edge is not. Count both before you trade.

What to notice

The price of an upgrade leaves your net worth in a single snapshot. The score sees that as a loss in the block where you bought it, whatever the upgrade earns later.

Remember

An upgrade is an investment: it must earn back its price inside the blocks that are left, or it is a drawdown you chose.

Act 4 · Badges

Badges: game facts, not grades

Where badges come from

Every badge is read from the recordings and the season files: an allocation, a block you survived, a venue you ran, a reaction time. None of them comes from a grade.

What you are looking at

One card per badge. The blue cards are facts many teams share; the gold cards are single awards, each with the number that earned it.

The season's badges

What to notice

The awards measure different things: speed on the flashes, one great block, steadiness across all blocks, the climb through the table, and cost control. They rarely go to the same team.

Remember

Each badge is a skill you can name in an interview, with the number behind it: how fast, how steady, how cheap.

The season

Season score = the mean of your scored block scores (a block needs at least snapshots). Season return = the block returns compounded. The change in net worth between two Fridays (open play, resets) never enters any return.

The idle-team line

Idle cash earns exactly the cash rate, so its excess return is zero and so is its score. That is the dashed zero line on every chart: a bar to its left did worse than parking the money in cash, once risk is counted.

Why a steady book wins

When your excess return is positive, a smaller volatility gives a larger ratio, and the drawdown term scales it down (a 25% drawdown halves it). Two books with the same return: the smoother one ranks higher.

The other side of the same formula: when a book earns less than cash, the ratio is negative, and a larger volatility makes it less negative. This season most blocks scored below zero for everybody, so the formula mostly ranked who lost least per unit of risk, and a nearly flat book that earned less than cash could rank below a book that lost more but moved more (as said in the week 6 recap). The drawdown term still makes every loss worse.
